1

What ChartVolt is, and where you fit

Understanding the model makes the rest of this document obvious rather than arbitrary.

ChartVolt runs paid skill competitions. A player buys credits, pays an entry fee to join a competition or to challenge another player one against one, competes, and the winners share the prize pool. Players earn points, ratings, levels, badges and milestones across everything they play.

All of that already exists and runs live. What we want to add is more things to compete at — chess puzzles, trivia, word games, logic puzzles, time-attack challenges. Rather than build them ourselves, we want to source them from partners like you.

The one thing that must be true of your games

Outcomes must be determined by player skill, not by chance. A player who is better at the game must reliably score higher. We cannot use games whose result comes from a random number generator, and we cannot use anything with a return-to-player percentage or a house edge. If your catalogue is chance-based, we are not the right partner for you and it is better that we both know now.

Two consequences you will like

You handle no money

No wallet integration, no balance callbacks, no bets, no payouts, no rollbacks, no reconciliation with us. We never ask you to debit or credit a player. This removes an entire category of integration work and compliance exposure from your side.

You see no personal data

We send you an opaque player identifier and, if you need it for the game UI, a display name. No email addresses, no real names, no dates of birth, no payment details, no identity documents.

4

Declaring what your games can do

We support two kinds of game, and each one needs different things from you.

“Independent play” is about your gameplay, not about how many people are competing

Every contest we run has at least two players, and a competition usually has many. There is no single-player paid format on our platform.

An independent-play game is simply one where each player plays their own round and gets their own score, rather than needing a live opponent in the same session. We then rank all of those players against each other. If your game can be played by one person at a time and produces a score, it is independent-play — and it will be used in contests of a hundred people.

Independent-play — family: "independent"

Each player plays their own round and receives a score. Trivia, puzzles, word games, chess puzzles, time attacks.

This is what we want most. An independent-play game works for both competitions and one-against-one challenges with no extra work from you, because we simply compare scores.

Head-to-head — family: "head_to_head"

The game needs an opponent to exist. Full chess, checkers, backgammon.

Useful for challenges, and we will need your optional match endpoint for it (section 18). Large competitions on these games are a later phase for us, so lead with independent-play titles if you have them.

If you have chess

A chess puzzle mode — solve as many as you can, or solve one as fast as you can — is far more valuable to us than full head-to-head chess, because it is independent-play and therefore works in a hundred-player competition immediately. If you can offer both, offer puzzles first.

Why we ask for real page content, not just a name

On ChartVolt, your game gets its own page — a header image, a description, the rules, a leaderboard of the best players, and the contests currently open on it. Players browse a catalogue of games and choose one before they see any competition. That page is where someone decides whether to pay an entry fee, so a name and a thumbnail are not enough to make it work.

ConstraintWhat it means
LocalisedText fields must be available in every locale the game declares. Either return a locale map, or honour an Accept-Language header on this endpoint. We will not translate your copy
No provider brandingDescriptions must not contain your company name, logo or links. To the player this is a ChartVolt game — contractual attribution is handled once in our footer and legal pages, not in game copy
Stable asset URLsHTTPS, with a cache policy. We cache and may re-host. URLs that rotate leave broken game pages behind
This is usually the cheapest thing you can give us

You already wrote this copy for your own storefront. Producing it per title, in every language, is inexpensive for you and expensive for us — and we will not launch a title we cannot present properly. Providers who supply good content get their games featured in our catalogue, which is a placement decision we make in minutes, not a development task.

One thing that will end the conversation early

We do not sell, and will not permit anything sold, that improves a score or ranking in a paid contest — no extra time, hints, retries, continues, or paid unlocks that affect results. This holds whoever takes the payment. Our contests award real prize money, and outcomes must be decided by skill alone. If a game's economics depend on those mechanics, it is the wrong game for us. Please raise it in your first reply rather than after an integration has been scoped.

Why configSchema is worth doing properly

We generate our admin settings form directly from your configSchema. If it is accurate and complete, you can add a new game to your catalogue and we can start running contests on it without writing or deploying any code. If it is vague, every new title needs developer time on our side, which makes us slow to adopt your releases. This one field largely determines how quickly your catalogue reaches our players.

Endpoint 4 — let us fetch a round

Our safety net. Do not skip this one.

GET{YOUR_BASE_URL}/v1/rounds/{roundId}Required

Return the current state of the round in exactly the same shape as the result callback body. If the round is still in progress, return it with a non-terminal status.

Why this is not negotiable

Webhooks get lost — to deployments, outages, misconfiguration and plain network faults. Without a way to ask you what happened, one lost message becomes a contest that can never be settled, with real prize money frozen inside it and players waiting. We poll this endpoint for any round still unresolved as a contest approaches settlement. A provider without it cannot be used for paid contests, regardless of how good the games are.

Authentication and signing

Both directions are authenticated and signed. We will exchange credentials before integration begins.

Calls from us to you

ItemValue
TransportHTTPS only, TLS 1.2 minimum
AuthenticationAuthorization: Bearer {API_KEY} — a key you issue to us
SignatureX-Signature: sha256={HMAC_SHA256(rawBody, API_SECRET)}
TimestampX-Timestamp — Unix seconds. Please reject anything older than 5 minutes
Our timeoutWe abort after 10 seconds and may retry

Your calls to us

ItemValue
AuthenticationAuthorization: Bearer {CALLBACK_TOKEN} — a token we issue to you
SignatureX-Signature: sha256={HMAC_SHA256(rawBody, CALLBACK_SECRET)}
TimestampX-Timestamp — Unix seconds. We reject anything older than 5 minutes
Signature basisThe raw request body bytes, exactly as sent

Signing, in Node.js

const crypto = require("crypto");

const body      = JSON.stringify(payload);   // serialise ONCE
const timestamp = Math.floor(Date.now() / 1000).toString();
const signature = crypto
  .createHmac("sha256", CALLBACK_SECRET)
  .update(body, "utf8")                      // sign that exact string
  .digest("hex");

await fetch(url, {
  method: "POST",
  headers: {
    "Content-Type":  "application/json",
    "Authorization": `Bearer ${CALLBACK_TOKEN}`,
    "X-Timestamp":   timestamp,
    "X-Signature":   `sha256=${signature}`
  },
  body                                        // send the SAME string
});

The same in PHP

$body      = json_encode($payload);
$timestamp = (string) time();
$signature = hash_hmac('sha256', $body, $CALLBACK_SECRET);

$headers = [
  'Content-Type: application/json',
  'Authorization: Bearer ' . $CALLBACK_TOKEN,
  'X-Timestamp: ' . $timestamp,
  'X-Signature: sha256=' . $signature,
];
The mistake that costs everyone a day of debugging

Sign the exact bytes you send, and send the exact bytes you signed. Serialise the payload to a string once, sign that string, and transmit that same string. If any layer re-serialises the object between signing and sending — a middleware, a logger, an HTTP client that takes an object rather than a string — key order or number formatting can shift and the signature will fail. The failures look intermittent and random, which is what makes them expensive. We verify against the raw body we receive, and we will do the same on our side.

Secret rotation

Please support rotating secrets without downtime, by accepting both the old and the new value during a changeover window. If a secret is ever exposed, rotation should be a configuration change rather than a coordinated release on both sides at once.

Idempotency and retries

Both sides will retry. Neither side should double-count anything.

SituationRequired behaviour
We call POST /v1/rounds twice with the same roundIdReturn the same round and the same launch URL. Do not create a second round. A player double-tapping Play must not consume two paid attempts
We call it with the same roundId but different parametersReject with 409. This means something is wrong and we would rather know than guess
You send the same eventId twiceWe record one score and reply 200 both times. Retrying is always safe for you
You send two different scores for one roundIdWe keep the first valid one and raise an internal alert. Please avoid this — it indicates a real defect
Your API is briefly unavailableWe retry idempotent calls up to 3 times with backoff, then fail the round without consuming the player's attempt
We are briefly unavailableRetry the result callback with backoff for at least 24 hours, same eventId
12

Identical content — the fairness requirement

The single most important guarantee in this document, and the one most often missing from existing APIs.

Every player in one competition must face identical content: the same questions, the same puzzles, the same starting position. We send you the same contentSeed for every round in a contest, and you must guarantee that the same seed with the same config produces the same content, every time.

What we need you to guarantee

Same contentSeed + same config → identical content, every time, indefinitely
Different contentSeed → different content
Presentation order may be shuffled per player; the underlying content may not change
The seed is never exposed to the player, in the page, the URL or any client-visible response
There is no endpoint anywhere that returns the content for a given seed
Content generated for a seed remains reproducible even after your content pool is updated
Why we care so much about this

Two players cannot be fairly ranked against each other if they answered different questions of unknown relative difficulty — and we are paying out real prize money on that ranking. Beyond fairness to players, identical conditions are what make relative skill rather than luck of the draw the deciding factor, which matters a great deal to how our product is classified and regulated. A game that cannot support seeding is still usable for free practice, but not for a paid competition.

13

Round states

Every round must reach one of four terminal states. There is no fifth option, and there is no "silence".

statusWhen to use itWhat we do
completedThe player finished the game normallyScore recorded and ranked
abandonedThe player quit or disconnected and did not returnScore recorded as reported. Counts as an attempt. Please send a partial score if you can compute one — a dropped mobile signal should not cost someone a paid entry
expiredexpiresAt passed before completionScored zero, or the partial score if you supply one
voidedYou invalidated the round — your own fault, a bug, or confirmed cheatingNot scored, and we return the attempt to the player. No money moves
A late result is better than no result

If something goes wrong and you cannot report within 60 seconds, still report. We keep a grace period after a contest closes precisely so that slow results are absorbed rather than lost, and even a result that arrives after settlement is valuable to us for auditing and for answering the player.

Errors

We need to know whether to retry, to fail, or to tell the player something.

{
  "error": {
    "code":      "GAME_UNAVAILABLE",
    "message":   "Game is in maintenance until 14:00 UTC.",
    "retryable": false
  }
}
HTTPMeaningWhat we do
400Invalid request, or config failing your own schemaFail immediately, no retry, and alert our team
401 403Auth or signature rejectedFail immediately, critical alert
404Unknown roundId or gameCodeFail immediately
409Same roundId, different parametersFail and alert — an identifier collision
422Valid shape, refused by your business rulesFail and show the player a message
429Rate limitedBack off. Please send Retry-After
5xxYour faultRetry up to 3 times, then fail the round without consuming the attempt
Two small things that help a lot

Always return JSON, never an HTML error page — an HTML body forces us to guess what went wrong. And use the retryable flag to tell us whether a 500 is transient or fatal; that single boolean is often the difference between recovering a player's round and wrongly consuming their paid attempt.

What we need from you to move forward

Answer these and we can tell you quickly whether there is a fit, before either side spends engineering time.

Technical

  1. Are your game outcomes decided by player skill or by a random number generator?
  2. Which of your games are independent-play, and which need an opponent?
  3. Can you support content seeding as described in section 12? If not today, could you?
  4. Do you have a pull endpoint for round state, or would it need building?
  5. How do you sign outbound webhooks today?
  6. What is your complete list of terminal round states, and can a round ever hang?
  7. Do you report partial scores for abandoned rounds?
  8. Do you provide replay links, and for how long do they stay valid?
  9. Do you provide anti-cheat or integrity signals?
  10. Does your sandbox let us force results?
  11. How do you version your API, and what notice do you give for breaking changes?

Product

  1. Full catalogue, with typical and maximum round duration for each game.
  2. Is there a practice mode?
  3. How large is the content pool per game, and how often is it refreshed?
  4. Which languages and platforms are supported?
  5. How often do you release new titles?
  6. Can games be visually themed to match our product?
  7. Do you supply page content per game — tagline, description, rules summary, how-to-play text — and in which languages?
  8. What artwork sizes do you provide, and are the URLs stable and cacheable?
  9. Does any game sell anything to the player during a round, and can that affect their score? If so, can it be disabled entirely?

Commercial and legal

  1. What is your pricing model, precisely? Per round, per active player, revenue share, or flat licence?
  2. Are practice rounds and abandoned rounds billed?
  3. Minimum commitment, contract term and notice period?
  4. What is your written SLA, and what are the remedies if it is missed?
  5. Which jurisdictions do you already operate in?
  6. Do you hold any fairness or game-testing certifications?
  7. Will you sign a data processing agreement, and where is data hosted?
  8. Who bears the cost if a scoring defect on your side causes us to pay a prize to the wrong player?
  9. Are you content for games to appear unbranded to players, with attribution in our footer and legal pages rather than in game copy?
On question 21

Pricing shapes what we can build together. Our most valuable contests for growth are low entry-fee, high-participation ones, and a per-round charge can make those uneconomic no matter how good the game is. A flat licence or a share of our platform fee lets us push volume aggressively, which is better for both of us. Tell us your model early so we can design contests around it rather than discovering the problem after launch.

The summary you can give your engineers
Build three endpoints — list games, create a round, fetch a round — and post us a signed score when a round ends. Make it idempotent, make every round always finish, and make one seed produce identical content for every player.
Everything else in this document is detail supporting those four sentences. No money, no player data, no knowledge of our competition mechanics required.
