Section 1

Purpose, Scope and Reservations

1.1This pack sets out ChartVolt's regulatory position and the evidence behind it, in a form a bank, an acquirer, a payment service provider or a competent authority can test. It addresses five perimeters: gambling and betting; investment services (MiFID II); electronic money (EMD2); payment services (PSD2); and anti-money-laundering (AMLD). For each it states the legal test, the facts of the platform that answer it, and the control in the code that keeps those facts true.

1.2Factual basis. Every factual statement about the platform in this pack was checked against ChartVolt's source code on 13 October 2026. Section 13 maps each control to the place it is enforced so it can be demonstrated live on request.

1.3Legal basis. The legal analysis follows ChartVolt's Preliminary Legal Opinion of 13 October 2026 and the instruments listed under References. Gambling law is not harmonised in the European Union, so the conclusions on gambling apply market by market. This pack is ChartVolt's statement of position; the formal opinion of Cyprus-qualified counsel, and of local counsel in each further market, is obtained separately and supersedes this pack where they differ.

1.4What "operated" means. Where this pack describes a control as operating in every live market, that is the configuration ChartVolt runs in production. Several controls are switchable by an administrator; the switch, the default and the place of enforcement are recorded in Section 13 so that the configuration can be verified rather than taken on trust.

Section 3

The Platform as Operated

3.1The following facts are established at source-code level and can be demonstrated to any authority.

  • Buying Volts. Players buy Volts at a published rate of 1 Volt = EUR 1, only through authorised payment service providers (Nuvei, Stripe, Paddle and Atlas). ChartVolt never receives funds outside regulated payment rails. Deposit and withdrawal fees are disclosed before payment.
  • Entering. A fixed entry fee, published before entry, buys a seat in a competition (two or more players) or a 1v1 challenge (exactly two). There is no paid single-player format. A competition below its minimum is cancelled and every fee refunded in full. Free practice is the only one-player mode, with no prize and no ranking.
  • Identical conditions. In a ranked contest every competitor receives the same content: the games service refuses to create a ranked round without the contest's shared seed, and generates from it the same puzzles, the same piece sequence or the same track. Simulated traders receive identical virtual capital and the same live price feed.
  • Scoring. Each title's score and its direction (higher wins, or fastest time wins) are fixed in the catalogue and published. A game provider reports a score only; it never touches money and cannot change how a game ranks.
  • Settlement. ChartVolt's settlement engine deducts the disclosed platform fee, ranks the players and pays prizes from the pool by the distribution table published before entry. Ties, players with no result and disqualification follow rules published in the competition entry terms.
  • Redemption. Volts can be redeemed for EUR only to the account holder, through the same authorised providers, after identity verification and the controls in Section 9.
  • No transfers. There is no feature by which one player can send, gift or sell Volts to another.

Section 4

Gambling and Betting — Skill, Not Chance

4A. Cyprus: outside the definition of betting

4.1The Cyprus Betting Law, Law 37(I)/2019, regulates betting by reference to wagers on events and outcomes at stated odds. ChartVolt differs on every element that makes an activity a bet: the player pays to perform an activity, not to predict someone else's; there are no odds; ChartVolt has no exposure to the result; and the result is the player's own measured performance.

4.2Not a casino-type game. The 2019 Law is generally understood to prohibit online casino-type games. None of ChartVolt's formats is one: there is no random-number draw that selects a winner, no card deal, no wheel, no reel and no house bank. Every title is a performance test in which the player's input determines the score.

4.3Peer-to-peer commission models. A commission-based peer-to-peer format is not automatically outside gambling law — Malta, for example, licenses peer-to-peer games of chance. The answer is that the commission structure is not what ChartVolt relies on. The reliance is on the absence of chance as the determinant of the winner, which Sections 4B–4D and 5 establish. A peer-to-peer structure without chance is a skill tournament, which is how chess opens, esports tournaments and fantasy leagues operate.

4.4The question in most jurisdictions is whether chance materially decides the winner, not whether any uncertainty exists. The UK Gambling Commission's guidance (persuasive, not binding in Cyprus) recognises that unpredictable elements a player must overcome can form part of a test of skill. ChartVolt's answer is engineered rather than argued: in a ranked contest every competitor faces the same problem, so whatever uncertainty the problem contains is common to all and cannot separate them. The only differentiating input is how each player plays it.

4D. Volt Velocity — the live race, controlled

4.5A live race is the format a regulator is most likely to question, because opponents share the track in real time. ChartVolt has removed each feature that could let something other than the pilot's own driving decide the result:

  • No player-versus-player items. EMP, shockwave, mines, oil slick, cloak and the blaster are removed. Shields, repairs, energy, overdrive, countermeasure and magnet affect only the collector. Opponents cannot be harmed, slowed or displaced by another racer's pickup.
  • Starting grid by standing, not shuffle. The grid is ordered by each racer's settled Volt Velocity points from earlier competitions and challenges, highest first, then by time of entry. It is read from the platform through a signed request before the countdown and cannot be changed during a race. If the platform cannot be reached, entry order applies — never a random draw.
  • Published disconnection rule. A disconnected ship stops while the clock runs. The racer has 30 seconds to reconnect; after that they are out with no score, and dropping out does not return the entry fee. A race cancelled before it starts is refunded in full. If a fault on ChartVolt's side stops a race, the contest's published rule for unfinished rounds applies. The rule appears in the game's rules, on the start screen and on the Help page, so every racer accepts it before paying.
How paid races are offered
Network quality is inherent to any live race; the disconnection rule makes its consequence known in advance. Paid Volt Velocity races are enabled only in markets where local counsel has confirmed the title's classification as a skill game. In any other market the title is offered free, through the same market-by-market gate described in Section 11.

4E. Why the payment of prizes changes nothing

4.6Paying money is not itself a licensable activity; the question is what the payment is. It is not investment proceeds (no instrument, no exposure, payout fixed per rank), and not gambling winnings (no odds, no stake multiplier, no chance determinant). It is a tournament prize: a fixed award from a pool published before entry, allocated by finishing position in a contest of skill. ChartVolt's revenue is the same whoever wins, and the prize is funded entirely by the entrants — ChartVolt carries no prize liability of its own and no exposure to any result.

Section 5

Skill Evidence from Real Data

5.1Regulators and acquirers ask the dominant-factor question in a measurable form: do skilled players demonstrably outperform unskilled players over repeated play? ChartVolt answers it from its own settled results. The administrative Skill Evidence report is computed from the final leaderboard of every settled contest, per title, for any date range an examiner chooses, and is exported as a dated PDF prepared for regulatory submission.

The report does not present a number on its own. Each measure is printed next to what the same field of players would produce if winners were drawn at random — the expected number of distinct winners, and the probability that any single player wins at least once — so an examiner can see the size of the departure from chance, not merely its direction. A field dominated by one repeat winner is identified as such rather than mistaken for randomness.

5.2Why this evidence is reliable. It is drawn from production data the platform already holds, not from a study commissioned for the purpose. It covers every settled contest in the period, not a selection. It can be regenerated on demand for a later period, so the evidence keeps pace with the catalogue, and it is reproducible: the same date range returns the same figures.

5.3Supply to authorities and partners. ChartVolt supplies the report for each Category A title, and for simulated trading, on request, together with the underlying aggregate counts. Individual players are not identified in the report.

Economic substance

6.2Supervisors assess novel products by economic substance: does the arrangement give the customer monetary exposure to the price movement of an underlying? It does not.

  • A CFD pays in proportion to the price move. A ChartVolt prize is a step function of finishing position: first place receives the same published prize whether the winning simulated return was 0.5% or 50%.
  • The maximum loss is the entry fee, fixed before entry; no market movement can increase it. There is no margin, no negative balance and no money at market risk.
  • The counterparty to each player's monetary outcome is the field of other entrants, through the pool — not ChartVolt and not the market.
  • Simulated profit is never paid out, in whole or in part. There is no profit-split, and no pathway from a competition to a live brokerage account.

6.3Regulatory posture. The Czech National Bank stated in 2024 that platforms operating wholly on simulated accounts do not require authorisation while they remain within the simulated model. Belgium's FSMA (2024) and Italy's CONSOB (2024) criticised paid trading "challenges" as consumer-protection matters without reclassifying them as investment services. Scrutiny in this sector has centred on profit-split "prop firm" models and bridges to live trading; ChartVolt has neither.

Position
ChartVolt performs no investment service in relation to any financial instrument and requires no MiFID II authorisation. The maintenance conditions are permanent product rules: no order routing, no bridge to live brokerage, and no payout that scales with simulated profit.
Section 7

Electronic Money and Payment Services

7A. Volts are not electronic money

7.1Under Article 2(2) of Directive 2009/110/EC, electronic money is monetary value issued on receipt of funds for the purpose of making payment transactions, and accepted by a natural or legal person other than the issuer. Both elements are cumulative. Volts fail the second completely:

  • Volts are issued by ChartVolt and accepted only by ChartVolt, for ChartVolt's own services — contest entries and items in ChartVolt's own marketplace.
  • No merchant, partner or other player accepts Volts. There is no feature by which a player can send, gift or sell Volts to anyone; the code contains no user-to-user transfer route.
  • A prize is not a payment from one player to another. Entrants each pay ChartVolt for a service; the winner receives a prize from ChartVolt out of the pool, under ChartVolt's published terms.
  • Game Master commission is a payment by ChartVolt from its own fee under a written agreement (Section 8), not acceptance of Volts by a third party in exchange for goods or services.

7.2Redemption does not create e-money. Redeemability is a duty imposed on e-money issuers; it is not what defines e-money. A prepaid balance that only its issuer accepts remains a prepayment for the issuer's services whether or not the issuer refunds it. ChartVolt's redemption returns a customer's own balance to that customer — no value reaches a third party at any point.

7B. Payment services — no payment service performed by ChartVolt

7.3PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) Annex I lists the regulated payment services, among them operating payment accounts, executing payment transactions and money remittance. Every movement of funds into and out of ChartVolt — purchase of Volts, redemption, refund — is executed by an authorised payment service provider under its own licence. ChartVolt does not hold customer funds on behalf of a payee, does not transmit funds to any third party and does not issue payment instruments.

7.4Limited-network exclusion, Article 3(k). PSD2 excludes instruments that can be used only to acquire goods or services in the issuer's premises or within a limited network of service providers under direct commercial agreement with the issuer. Volts fit its purpose exactly: they are usable only on ChartVolt, for ChartVolt's services.

7.5Redemption is a refund to source, not a remittance. Money remittance requires funds to be transmitted to a payee. When a ChartVolt customer redeems Volts, the payer and the payee are the same verified person and, in every live market, the funds return to the original payment method that funded the account. In substance the redemption is a refund of an unused prepayment — a familiar consumer-protection feature — and it moves no value to anyone else. The controls around it (Section 9) ensure it cannot be used as a store of value for third-party payments:

  • identity verification (KYC) before any redemption, with duplicate-identity detection;
  • a minimum-deposit rule and a hold period after deposit, so funds cannot be paid in and immediately out;
  • redemption only to the original payment method, so value cannot be redirected to another person or account;
  • manual review of every withdrawal request, with per-request, daily and monthly limits and a cap on requests per day;
  • a disclosed withdrawal fee, which removes any incentive to use the balance as a transit account.

7C. Article 37(2) — monitored, with a notification route

7.6A provider relying on the limited-network exclusion must notify its competent authority — in Cyprus, the Central Bank of Cyprus — if the total value of payment transactions under the exclusion over the preceding 12 months exceeds EUR 1 million. ChartVolt monitors that figure on a rolling 12-month basis from its financial ledger, which records every purchase of Volts and every redemption with its amount and date. If the threshold is approached, ChartVolt will file the Article 37(2) notification with the Central Bank of Cyprus and, if useful, use the pre-application exploratory stage the Central Bank introduced on 3 August 2026 to settle the classification of Volts with the authority directly.

Position
Volts are not electronic money, because no one other than ChartVolt accepts them. ChartVolt performs no payment service, because authorised providers execute every transfer of funds, and redemptions return a customer's own balance to their own original payment method. Volts fall within the limited-network exclusion of Article 3(k) PSD2, and the Article 37(2) threshold is monitored with a defined notification route.

Some member states ask whether skill predominates; others weigh any material element of chance; some restrict paid skill games with cash prizes specifically. How the gate works

  • An administrator maintains the list of restricted countries. Paid entry is blocked for those countries; registration can be blocked as well.
  • The check uses two independent signals: the country of the network connection, supplied at the edge of ChartVolt's infrastructure, and the country the player declared at registration. Either signal falling in a restricted country blocks the action.
  • The gate is applied at registration, competition entry, challenge creation and challenge acceptance — every point at which a paid commitment is made.
  • Connections whose country cannot be determined can be treated as restricted, and VPN and proxy detection (Section 9) catches attempts to disguise location.
Position
Paid competition is offered only where it has been reviewed and is technically blocked everywhere else. A new market is opened by a deliberate decision recorded against a legal review, never by default.

Q1Are you a gambling merchant?
No. Every competitor receives identical content, no random draw decides the winner, nothing can be bought to improve a score, and prizes are paid by rank from a pool published before entry. The Skill Evidence report (Section 5) shows from production data that results are stable for repeat players, improve with experience and concentrate among fewer winners than chance would produce.
Q2Are you a trading, CFD or prop-firm merchant?
No. No customer money is exposed to any market; the trading is a simulation and the most a player can lose is a fixed entry fee. No payout tracks simulated profit, there is no profit-split, and no order or account connects to a broker.
Q3Where is your licence?
None is required: ChartVolt performs no investment service (Section 6), offers no game of chance (Section 4), issues no electronic money and performs no payment service (Section 7). Paid entry is restricted to markets individually reviewed (Section 11). Counsel opinions are supplied for underwriting files on request.
Q4Is the Volts balance a stored-value or e-money product?
No. Only ChartVolt accepts Volts, for its own services; they cannot be sent to another user or spent with another merchant. A redemption returns the customer's own balance to the original payment method, after KYC, a hold period and manual review. Volts fall within the PSD2 limited-network exclusion and ChartVolt monitors the Article 37(2) threshold.
Q5What are your AML and source-of-funds controls?
Funds enter only through authorised payment providers. KYC through Veriff is required before any redemption, with duplicate-identity detection; a minimum-deposit rule and hold period apply; redemptions go only to the original payment method; every withdrawal is manually reviewed against limits. Device fingerprinting, multi-account detection, IP intelligence and suspicion scoring run continuously.
Q6What does the cardholder buy, and what do you pay out?
The cardholder buys Volts, used for fixed contest entry fees and ChartVolt marketplace items — a service, VAT-invoiced. Payouts are tournament prizes by rank from fully funded pools, and redemptions of a customer's own balance to their own payment method. Prize liability is always covered by the entry fees collected; ChartVolt carries no open-ended liability and no exposure to any result.
Q7How do you handle chargebacks and refunds?
Dispute notifications from the payment provider are ingested automatically, the account is restricted, and a case file with identity, device, IP, entry and ledger evidence is assembled for representment; outcomes reconcile into the ledger. Contests that do not start or are cancelled refund automatically in full. Declined payments never credit an account.
Q8Which countries do you serve?
Paid entry is enabled only in individually reviewed markets and blocked elsewhere using the network country and the declared country together, at registration, entry and challenge creation and acceptance. ChartVolt will not open a market whose law would require a 7995 classification.

Section 15

Formal Statement of Position

Re: Regulatory classification of ChartVolt's skill-competition and simulated-trading platform.

15.1Investment services. ChartVolt's simulated-trading competitions are not investment services within Annex I Section A of Directive 2014/65/EU. Participants acquire no financial instruments, place no executable orders, entrust no assets and receive no payment representing an investment return; prizes are allocated by rank from a pool funded by entry fees.

15.2Betting. ChartVolt's competitions are not betting under Law 37(I)/2019: participants pay to perform an activity rather than to wager on an event, no odds are offered, the operator has no exposure to the outcome, and prizes are determined by the participant's measured performance.

15.3Games of chance. In every title offered for paid entry, each competitor in a contest receives identical, deterministically generated content, no advantage can be purchased, and the winner is decided by a published fixed score rule. The outcome is determined predominantly by skill, which is supported by ChartVolt's own skill evidence. Volt Velocity races contain no player-versus-player items, start from a grid ordered by prior standing and operate a published reconnection rule; they are offered for paid entry where local counsel has confirmed that classification.

15.4Electronic money and payment services. Volts are issued and accepted exclusively by ChartVolt for its own services and cannot be transferred between users. They do not meet the third-party-acceptance element of Article 2(2) of Directive 2009/110/EC and fall within the limited-network exclusion in Article 3(k) of Directive (EU) 2015/2366. Every transfer of funds is executed by an authorised payment service provider; redemptions return a customer's own balance to their original payment method after identity verification. Game Master commission is a commercial payment by ChartVolt from its own fee under a written agreement. The Article 37(2) threshold is monitored.

15.5Anti-money-laundering. ChartVolt is not an obliged entity under Directive (EU) 2015/849, and operates identity verification, refund-to-source, hold, review, fraud and chargeback controls proportionate to a closed-loop platform with no transfers between users.

Overall
ChartVolt is a provider of skill-based competitive entertainment and simulated performance assessment — not of investment, betting, gambling, electronic-money or payment services. Paid entry is offered only in individually reviewed markets, with the controls set out in this pack operating in each of them.
