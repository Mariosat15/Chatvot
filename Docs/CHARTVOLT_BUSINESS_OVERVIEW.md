# ChartVolt — Business Overview

*A clear, plain-language summary of what ChartVolt is, who it serves, how it makes
money, how it protects the company, the bank, and the customer, and how it plans to grow.*

> **Note on numbers.** All fees, limits, and rates in this document are the platform's
> current configurable defaults (set by the operator in the admin panel). They can be
> changed per brand/white-label. The financial forecast in Section 8 is an **illustrative
> model** built on stated assumptions — it is a planning tool, not a guarantee.

---

## 1. What Is the Product

**ChartVolt is a gamified trading competition platform.** Users trade against **real,
live market prices** using **virtual capital** — so they get the full thrill and skill of
trading **without risking money on the markets themselves**. Instead, they compete for
prize pools in tournaments and head-to-head duels.

Think of it as **"fantasy sports for traders"**: real-time data, real skill, real prizes —
but the financial risk is capped at a small, known entry fee rather than open market exposure.

**Core experience:**

| Pillar | What it is |
|--------|-----------|
| **Live trading simulator** | Real-time forex prices (bid/ask), leverage, stop-loss/take-profit, margin and liquidation — a realistic trading engine, fully simulated. |
| **Competitions** | Multi-player tournaments with an entry fee, a shared prize pool, a live leaderboard, and automated start/finish. |
| **1v1 Challenges** | Head-to-head duels — two traders, winner takes the pot (minus a small platform fee). |
| **Global leaderboard** | Platform-wide ranking by performance, wins, and achievements. |
| **Marketplace** | Spend in-app credits on trading indicators, strategies, cosmetics, and creator packages. |
| **Game Master program** | Power users buy a package to host their own competitions and earn referral income. |
| **Progression system** | Trader levels, titles, badges, and a "journey" map that rewards engagement over time. |
| **Learning** | In-app tutorials and a detailed Help Center so new users ramp up fast. |

**The wallet at the center of it all:** users deposit money, receive **Volt Credits**
(currency of the platform), and use those credits to enter competitions, buy items, and
withdraw winnings.

## 2. Who Is the Target Audience

**Primary audiences:**

1. **Aspiring & retail traders** who want to sharpen their skills and compete — but are
   nervous about (or priced out of) risking real capital on live markets.
2. **Competitive gamers / esports-minded users** who are drawn to leaderboards, prizes,
   ranks, and head-to-head play more than to investing per se.
3. **Trading-content creators & communities** (the "Game Masters") who want to host
   contests for their audience and earn from engagement.

**Secondary / B2B audience:**

4. **Brokers, trading educators, and fintech brands** who want a ready-made, branded
   competition product — ChartVolt is built **white-label**, so a partner can run the
   entire platform under their own name and branding.

**Demographic profile:** digitally native, 18–40, mobile-first, already active in trading
apps, crypto, sports betting, or competitive gaming — comfortable with online payments and
motivated by skill-based competition.

## 3. What Is the Opportunity

Three large trends overlap exactly where ChartVolt sits:

- **The retail trading boom** — millions of new retail traders have entered the market,
  but most lose money and churn quickly because the **risk and learning curve are brutal**.
- **The gamification & skill-gaming wave** — competitive, prize-based gaming and fantasy
  formats are mainstream and highly monetizable.
- **Demand for "safe" ways to trade** — there is a clear gap for a product that delivers
  the excitement of trading with a **fixed, small, known cost** instead of unlimited losses.

**ChartVolt's wedge:** it converts an activity that normally **loses users money and trust**
into one that is **fun, social, capped-risk, and repeatable**. That improves retention and
lifetime value versus a typical brokerage funnel — and it sidesteps much of the regulatory
weight of handling real market execution, because trading is simulated.

**Add the white-label angle**, and ChartVolt is not just one consumer app — it is a
**platform other brands can license**, multiplying the addressable market.

**What we target (product focus):**

  creator economy, and deepen the progression/journey systems that drive retention.

**Where we target (markets):**

  automatically based on the customer's country and the operating entity.
- **Phase 3 — B2B / white-label:** sign brand and broker partners who bring their own
  audiences, turning ChartVolt into the infrastructure behind many branded front-ends.

## 5. How We Market

**Acquisition (getting users in):**

- **Creator-led growth (lowest cost, highest trust):** the **Game Master program** turns
  influencers and community leaders into distribution. They host competitions for their
  followers and earn a **referral share (default 5%)** — they bring the audience, we provide
  the platform.
- **Performance marketing:** targeted social/search ads aimed at retail-trading and
  competitive-gaming audiences, with "trade with no market risk, win real prizes" messaging.
- **Tournaments as events:** flagship competitions with headline prize pools create
  marketing moments, PR, and viral leaderboards.

**Activation & retention (keeping them):**

- **Free-to-try funnel** — low minimum deposit (default **€10**) lowers the barrier to entry.
- **Progression hooks** — badges, 20 trader levels/titles, and the journey map give users
  reasons to return daily.
- **Social competition** — global leaderboards and 1v1 challenges create rivalry and repeat play.
- **Education** — in-app tutorials and Help Center reduce early churn from confusion.

**Partnerships:**

- **White-label deals** with brokers/educators bring whole communities at once.
- **Affiliate / referral mechanics** are built into the product, not bolted on.

## 6. How We Protect — Company, Bank & Customer

Protection is built into the platform at every layer. This is a core strength, not an afterthought.

### Protecting the Company
- **Fraud & abuse detection:** device fingerprinting, suspicion scoring, fraud alerts, and
  multi-account / same-IP detection to stop bonus abuse and collusion.
- **Fair-play / anti-cheat:** anti-mirror-trading and drawdown rules inside competitions,
  plus price-snapshot fallbacks so results can always be settled fairly.
- **Audit logs:** every sensitive admin action is recorded with IP and device for accountability.
- **Financial reconciliation:** an automated service continuously checks that every wallet
  balance matches its transaction history and flags any discrepancy.

### Protecting the Bank / Payment Channel
- **Webhook signature verification:** all payment confirmations are cryptographically
  verified (HMAC) before any credit is granted — forged payment messages are rejected and logged.
- **Idempotent payments:** a deposit can never be double-counted, even on repeated provider callbacks.
- **Chargeback defense system:** when a chargeback is filed, the platform auto-generates a
  **professional evidence report (downloadable document)** with the transaction trail, device,
  KYC, and consent data needed to dispute it — and automatically suspends the account and
  claws back the disputed credits.
- **Rate limiting & velocity checks** on deposits and withdrawals to block automated attacks.

### Protecting the Customer
- **Capped risk by design:** a user can only ever lose their (small) entry fee — never an
  uncapped market loss. This is the core consumer-safety promise.
- **Identity verification (KYC):** configurable identity checks (via Veriff), required for
  withdrawals by default, protecting both the user and the platform from impersonation and theft.
- **Withdrawal safeguards:** minimums, daily/monthly limits, optional 2FA, and hold periods
  guard against account takeover draining funds.
- **Clear money trail:** every transaction (deposit, fee, entry, win, withdrawal) is itemized,
  with automatic invoices and correct VAT, so customers always see exactly what they paid.
- **Injection & input defenses** across login and payment endpoints to protect user accounts and data.

## 7. Flow of Funds

Money moves through the platform in a clear, tracked loop. Every step writes a ledger entry.

```
 1. DEPOSIT
    Customer pays by card (primary processor: Nuvei; Stripe also supported)
          │   + platform deposit fee (default 2%)  + VAT where applicable (default 21%)
          ▼
 2. CREDITS ISSUED
    Volt Credits added to the user's wallet   (default rate: 100 credits = €1)
          │
          ▼
 3. PLAY
    Credits spent on:  competition entry  ·  1v1 challenge stake
                       marketplace items  ·  Game Master packages
          │
          ▼
 4. PRIZES
    Winners receive credits from the prize pool, minus the platform fee
    (competitions: 20% default · challenges: 10% default)
          │
          ▼
 5. WITHDRAWAL
    User cashes out credits → EUR  (min €20; daily/monthly limits; KYC + checks)
          │   − platform withdrawal fee (default 2%)
          ▼
    Payout to the customer's bank/card
```

**Where the platform earns revenue (all configurable):**

| Revenue source | Default |
|----------------|---------|
| Deposit fee | **2%** on deposits |
| Withdrawal fee | **2%** on withdrawals |
| Competition fee | **20%** of the prize pool |
| 1v1 challenge fee | **10%** of the prize pool |
| Marketplace sales | full credit price of the item |
| Game Master package sales | package price (set by operator) |
| Unclaimed prize pools | retained when a contest has no eligible winner |
| Retained creator fees | retained when a Game Master subscription lapses |

> The platform makes money from **fees and product sales**, **not** from users' trading
> losses (trading is simulated; there is no spread/commission rake on trades). Revenue scales
> with **activity and engagement**, which aligns the platform's success with the user having fun.

**Behind the scenes:** separate ledgers track the **customer wallet** and the **platform's
own revenue**, an automated **reconciliation** service guarantees they always agree, and
**refunds/chargebacks** are handled with their own transaction types so the books stay clean.

## 8. Growth Plan & Turnover Forecast

### 8a. Growth Plan (phased)

| Phase | Focus | Key moves |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| **Phase 1 — Launch & prove** | One core market, forex competitions & 1v1 | Onboard first Game Masters, run flagship tournaments, tighten retention loops |
| **Phase 2 — Scale** | Paid acquisition + creator network | Expand regions, multi-language, broaden marketplace, add asset classes |
| **Phase 3 — Platform** | White-label & B2B | License the platform to brokers/brands; each partner brings its own audience |

**Operational readiness for growth:** the platform already runs a **multi-server architecture**
(a primary server handling the live price feed and core processing, with secondary servers that
scale user/web traffic via a Redis relay). This means **user-facing capacity can be scaled
horizontally by adding servers** — the system is built to grow without a re-architecture.

### 8b. Turnover Forecast — *Illustrative Model*

> **This is a planning model, not a promise.** It shows how turnover behaves given a set of
> assumptions you can adjust. Replace the assumptions with your own market data before using
> it for fundraising or budgeting.

**Assumptions (editable):**

- **ARPU (average revenue per active user / month):** derived from fees on deposits,
  withdrawals, and contest activity. Model assumes **€8 / active user / month** blended.
- **Average monthly deposit per active user:** ~€60 (drives fee revenue).
- **Monthly churn / activity** baked into the "active users" figure.
- **Blended take rate** across all fee sources roughly in line with the defaults in Section 7.

**Illustrative trajectory:**

| Period | Avg. monthly active users | Monthly platform revenue (≈ €8 ARPU) | Annualized turnover |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------------------------------|---------------------|
| Year 1 (ramp) | 2,000 | ~€16,000 | **~€190K** |
| Year 2 (scale) | 10,000 | ~€80,000 | **~€960K** |
| Year 3 (platform + WL) | 30,000 | ~€240,000 | **~€2.9M** |

*Note: turnover here means **platform revenue** (fees + sales), not gross payment volume.
Gross transaction volume flowing through the wallet is several times larger.*

**What moves these numbers most (the levers):**

1. **Active users** — the single biggest driver; powered by the creator/Game Master network.
2. **Engagement per user** — more contests played = more fee revenue at the same headcount.
3. **Deposit size & frequency** — drives deposit/withdrawal fee income.
4. **White-label partners** — each partner is a step-change in users without linear ad spend.

### 8c. Cost & Margin Notes

- **Payment processing** (card fees ~2.9% + €0.30 per deposit, payout fees) is the main
  variable cost — partly offset by the platform's own deposit/withdrawal fees.
- **Infrastructure** scales gradually (add servers as users grow) and is a modest share of revenue.
- **Marketing** is the main growth investment — kept efficient by leaning on creator-led,
  referral-based acquisition rather than pure paid ads.
